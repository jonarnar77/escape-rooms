# The Town That Erased You

You come home to Little Wending after twelve years away. Nobody knows you. Your name is gone from
every record, strangers live in your house, and the map you drew as a child with your sibling Wren
is fading off the paper.

Tone: warm autumn daylight, small market town by the sea. Melancholy and gentle; eerie only at the edges.

## Rules (lessons from the hotel)

1. **One location = one room.** One main screen, a few close-ups, two or three puzzles.
2. **Every clue is inside the room it is used in.** Nothing needs notes carried between rooms.
   The only exception is the last room, which uses the six keepsakes, and says so.
3. **The map is a reward, not a hub.** Each room ends with a keepsake (a memory). Taking it redraws
   one street on the map, which leads to the next room. The order is fixed.
4. **The sibling story is told, not solved.** Green ink, a neat hand and the initial W. turn up in every
   room; the notebook records them automatically. No puzzle depends on them.
5. **Every gate says what it wants**, and easy mode sparkles the next step.

## Characters

- **Robin Ashby** (you). Left at nineteen, promised to come back for the summer, never did.
- **Wren Ashby**, the younger sibling who stayed. Now keeps the clock tower and the town records.
  Wren has been erasing you, quietly and neatly, in green ink. Not from spite: from grief.
- **Mum**, who kept your post. (Gone now; the house was sold.)

## Flow

Station (intro) → map → 1 Post office → map → 2 Café → … → 6 Clock tower.

### 0. Station and the map
The train leaves you on the platform. The welcome sign says *pop. 2,413*, with the 3 changed to a 2 in
green ink. Your childhood map (drawn with Wren) is in your bag. Only Station Road is still on it.

### 1. Post office: *the promise*
- Staff hatch: 4-digit code. The reminder on the counter says the code is today's date (DDMM); the date
  stamp on the counter shows it. Easy: format spelled out. Hard: random date.
- Behind the counter, the post is unsorted: put six letters into their streets' pigeonholes (assign).
  Medium/hard use abbreviated addresses.
- One letter is stuck behind the rack, for *Lantern Row*, which has no pigeonhole. A green label covers
  the name: NO SUCH PERSON. Steam it open at the kettle: it is from Mum, twelve years ago, with your
  post-box key (tag 7). Your name comes back: Robin Ashby.
- Box 7 is the one painted over. Inside: every postcard you sent home. The first says *Tell Wren I'll be
  home for the summer. Promise.* **Keepsake: the postcard.** Harbour Lane redraws.

### 2. The Marigold café: *Saturday mornings*
- The staff photo over the counter (*Saturday staff, 2013*) has a neat, repainted gap.
- The guest book on the window table holds the original print. Dot's caption, *"Robin's last Saturday!"*,
  has *Robin's* scribbled out in green ink.
- With the print, the photo close-up gets a **Then / Now** button (`canPast` on the scene). Three things are
  missing from the wall copy: you (holding your mug: chipped rim, blue stripe), *ROBIN'S* flapjacks on the
  chalkboard, and your name on the Saturday rota.
- Three mugs on the rail have their names scraped off (four on hard). Pick yours.
- Inside: a map Wren drew the day before you left, *"So you don't forget the way home. W."*
  **Keepsake: Wren's drawing.** Press Street redraws.

### 3. The Wending Gazette: *the night you found Wren*
- The framed front page for 18 August 2009 is a REPRINT: a filler headline about bins in the wrong typeface,
  and a hole where the photograph was. Weather SUNNY 21°, advert Penhale's Bakery.
- The docket on the spike, in green ink: *"Story withdrawn at the family's request. W. Ashby, Town Records."*
  (The eraser shares your surname.) The typewriter holds an unfinished notice: *"Lost:"*.
- The printing plates read backwards: take the compositor's mirror. Pick the plate that matches the reprint
  except for the story; its date is scraped off. Easy: one scraped plate. Medium: two (weather differs).
  Hard: three (weather and advert both needed).
- The press: plate in the bed, ink on the disc, pull the lever. Out comes *FOUND AT DAWN*: Wren, 11, lost
  in the storm, found by Robin, 14, at the old lighthouse. **Keepsake: the front page.** Market Street redraws.

### 4. Town hall: *named for birds*
- The strongroom has a time lock that follows the master clock, stopped at 4.20: after hours.
  The records office sign gives the hours (9 till 4, closed 1 till 2; hard: 10 till 3.30, closed
  12.30 till 1.30). Set the master clock to any time inside them (clock widget with `check`).
- Notice board, in green ink: *"Records are moving to the clock tower archive. W. Ashby."* The official
  town plan has Lantern Row painted out in green.
- Strongroom: a reading slip, *"Withdrawn: Births 1995, p. 23. Replaced. W.A."* Page 23 is a neat
  replacement in green ink, with a cut stub in the binding.
- Rub the pencil from the counter over the blank page 24: the pressed-in original appears.
  *ASHBY, Robin. Remarks: named for the robin at the window.* **Keepsake: the rubbing.**
  Memory: Mum named us for the robin and the wren at the kitchen window. *"Birds always come home."*
  Lantern Row redraws.

### 5. Old home, Lantern Row: *the night you left*
- Strangers live there now and are out. Their note on the navy door: the key is under the robin
  (hard: "the usual bird, the one over the door": the robin in the stained-glass fanlight).
  Four stone birds in the garden: wren, robin, gull, duck.
- Inside, everything is freshly white. Ridges under the paint on the kitchen door frame: scrape them
  with the decorators' scraper. Mum's height marks, R and W, every birthday. R stops at 19.
  Above it, a dashed green line: *R 20?* Easy/medium write the heights; hard only has Mum's scale
  (and a random height, a multiple of 5).
- Cupboard under the stairs: the loose board, R and W's hiding place. The old biscuit tin, 3-digit lock,
  green tape: *"How tall you were the night you went. W."*
- Inside: R's mix tape for W, the robin badge, Wren's list (*"Things I couldn't throw away"*) and the note
  you left on Wren's pillow: *the 4.20 train... I'll be back for the summer. Wind the tower clock for
  me till then?* Under it, in green: *"I wound it every day. Then the summer ended, and I stopped it at
  twenty past four. W."* **Keepsake: your note.** Tower Steps redraws.

### 6. Clock tower: *the goodbye* (sketch)
Wren's room, full of green ink. The tower clock stopped the summer you didn't come. Use the six keepsakes
to set it going again. Wren is there. An ending, not an escape.
